## Known limitation of the fitted model (documented, not hidden)

The Step 10 generator's burst model is degenerate on this dense data:
train gaps average 23.1 s, so 99.27% of gaps are ≤ 300 s and the fitted
burst probability is 0.9927. The generator therefore draws ~99% of gaps
from `Uniform(0, 300)` (mean 150 s) instead of the exponential(23.1 s),
so synthetic streams are ~6× sparser in time than train (expected mean
gap ≈ 0.9927×150 + 0.0073×23 ≈ 149 s vs 23.1 s train). Consequences:

- Synthetic workloads remain valid **systems** workloads: both engines
  read the identical generated bytes, and the measured cost is the cost
  of the same pipeline on the same input. Ordering claims are about
  engine cost on identical bytes, not about real-world event density.
- Synthetic window counts per event will be lower than real (longer
  spans per event → fewer events per history window, more origins per
  event). Do not compare synthetic and real window counts as if the
  densities matched; the report keeps the two families separate.
- The hour weights influence gap stretching only (they are not sampled
  directly), so the realized synthetic hour distribution is flatter
  than the fitted weights; the aggregate comparison below is the
  check the kit asks for, not a claim of distributional equality.

workloads on identical bytes, not a distributional replica of train.
